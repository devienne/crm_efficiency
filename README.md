# Efficient Remanence Recording During Magnetite Growth Through the Vortex Transition 

Magnetite formed at constant temperature acquires a crystallization remanent magnetization (CRM) as particles grow through their blocking volume, beyond which their magnetic states become stable on geological timescales. Classical CRM theory focused on uniformly magnetized single-domain particles and has been interpreted to suggest that reliable paleointensity estimates require millions or billions of grains. However, many paleomagnetically relevant minerals in terrestrial and extraterrestrial materials adopt non-uniform vortex states, whose CRM can exceed the thermoremanent magnetization acquired by equivalent particles at higher temperatures. Here, we reassess the statistical efficiency of CRM recording by magnetite particles growing from the single-domain into the vortex size range. We show that these particles acquire a statistically robust net remanence closely aligned with the applied field. Under an Earth-like fields (~30-50 μT), only a few thousand particles are required to recover paleofield direction and intensity reliably. Fields as weak as < 1-10 μT remain detectable, although requiring larger number of particles. Our results identify CRM-bearing materials as promising targets for quantum diamond microscopy and dipole-fitting paleomagnetic reconstructions.
